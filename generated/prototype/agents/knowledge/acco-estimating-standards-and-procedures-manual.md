# ACCO Estimating Standards and Procedures Manual

**Organization:** ACCO Engineered Systems
**Industry:** Mechanical Construction & Facility Services
**Last Updated:** May 18, 2026
**Classification:** Internal Use

---

## Overview

This manual defines the internal standards every ACCO Engineered Systems
estimator follows when preparing a bid. ACCO operates across the western
United States as a single-source provider of engineered mechanical systems
for data centers, healthcare, pharmaceutical and life sciences, sports and
entertainment venues, commercial offices, higher education campuses, and
government facilities. Because our scopes routinely cross HVAC, piping,
plumbing, controls, and commissioning, estimators must produce structured
estimates that allow Project Management, Procurement, and Risk Review to
read a bid the same way every time.

This manual is the source of truth for estimate structure, mark-up rules,
approval thresholds, exclusions, and clarifications. It is referenced by
the Scope Analysis Agent inside the ACCO Estimator Assistant and by the
pre-construction team during weekly bid review.

## 1. Estimate Structure

Every ACCO estimate is organized into five top-level cost categories. Do
not invent new categories — if a scope item does not fit, escalate to the
Chief Estimator for a categorical decision.

1. **HVAC** — sheet metal, piping, insulation, equipment installation.
2. **Plumbing** — domestic water, drainage, medical gas, process piping.
3. **Controls** — BMS, sequence of operations, integration with EPMS.
4. **Equipment Procurement** — long-lead chillers, boilers, AHUs, CRAH units.
5. **Commissioning** — startup, test & balance, IQ/OQ documentation.

Within each category, estimators break out labor, material, equipment,
subcontract, and general conditions on separate lines. The summary sheet
must reconcile to the line items within ±$50.

### 1.1 Labor unit pricing

Labor lines must use the productivity factors published in the *Trade
Productivity Reference Guide*. Estimators do not invent productivity values.
Where a scope is unusual (e.g. confined-space welding, off-shift work),
add a productivity multiplier with a note explaining the assumption.

### 1.2 Material and equipment

Material pricing uses live vendor quotes obtained within the past 60 days.
Equipment over $250,000 list price requires a written vendor quote attached
to the bid file. Lead-time risk is annotated next to the equipment line and
echoed in the Schedule Risk section.

## 2. Mark-up Rules

Mark-up applies to the fully burdened cost of each cost category.

| Cost band            | Default mark-up | Approver           |
|----------------------|-----------------|--------------------|
| Under $500,000       | 18%             | Senior Estimator   |
| $500,000 – $2M       | 14%             | Estimating Manager |
| $2M – $10M           | 11%             | Chief Estimator    |
| $10M – $50M          | 9%              | VP Pre-Construction|
| Over $50M            | 8% (floor)      | President + Risk   |

Owner-furnished, contractor-installed (OFCI) equipment is marked up at half
the table rate. Self-perform labor carries an additional 2% risk premium
above the table rate on any contract with a duration over 18 months.

## 3. Approval Thresholds

The Senior Estimator owns bids under $500K. Above that, the Estimating
Manager must sign the summary sheet. Above $2M, a written risk memo from
the Chief Estimator must be attached to the proposal package. Above $10M
or any bid involving a new client, the VP Pre-Construction is the named
approver and an Executive Risk Review is scheduled at least 5 business
days before the proposal due date.

### 3.1 Cycle time

Standard bids are returned within 10 business days of RFP receipt. Bids
above $10M get a 15-business-day cycle. Same-day or 48-hour requests are
declined unless approved in writing by the VP Pre-Construction.

## 4. Exclusions

The following items are excluded from every ACCO bid unless explicitly
included in writing by the customer. Estimators copy the relevant exclusion
language verbatim into the bid response.

- Cutting, patching, and core-drilling of structural elements
- Fireproofing, fire-stopping, and intumescent paint
- Painting of mechanical equipment, ductwork, or piping unless specifically
  scoped in the contract documents
- Excavation, dewatering, shoring, and backfill
- Electrical work beyond final connections to mechanical equipment
- Roofing, flashing, and curbs for rooftop equipment
- Permits beyond mechanical, plumbing, and refrigeration permits
- Owner-furnished long-lead equipment unless installation is explicitly scoped
- Asbestos, lead, mold, or hazardous-material abatement
- Builder's risk insurance and performance/payment bonds beyond ACCO's
  standard inclusion limits
- Overtime, premium time, or shift work unless specifically scheduled in
  the bid
- Cybersecurity hardening of BMS controllers beyond manufacturer defaults

## 5. Clarifications

Clarifications signal the assumptions under which the bid is valid. Common
clarifications include:

- Bid pricing valid for 30 days from submission
- Equipment escalation beyond 6 months is the customer's risk
- Labor rates valid through current collective bargaining agreement term
- ACCO will provide one project manager and one site superintendent; all
  additional management staffing requires a change order
- Working hours assumed Monday–Friday, 6 AM to 3 PM, no overtime included
- Site access assumed continuous from notice-to-proceed through substantial
  completion

## 6. Quality Gates

Every bid passes through three gates before submission:

1. **Peer review** — A second estimator runs the summary sheet and confirms
   the labor unit pricing matches the productivity guide.
2. **PM review** — The Project Management group reviews the schedule
   assumption and the constructability of the labor hours.
3. **Risk review** — Risk reviews exclusions, clarifications, and any
   non-standard contract terms (LDs, retention, indemnity).

A bid that does not clear all three gates is not submitted.

## 7. Estimating Tools

Estimators use ACCO's internal cost database, the *Trade Productivity
Reference Guide*, and the *RFP Response Playbook*. The ACCO Estimator
Assistant AI copilot is approved for first-pass research, comparable-project
lookups, and risk flag identification. It is **not** approved as the sole
source for a labor unit price or mark-up decision — those remain the
estimator's responsibility.

## 8. Document Control

This manual is reviewed annually by the Chief Estimator and the VP
Pre-Construction. Revisions are tracked in the Pre-Construction shared
library. Estimators are responsible for confirming they are working from
the current version before publishing a bid.
