# RFP Response Playbook

**Organization:** ACCO Engineered Systems
**Industry:** Mechanical Construction & Facility Services
**Last Updated:** May 18, 2026
**Classification:** Internal Use

---

## Overview

This playbook standardizes how ACCO Engineered Systems analyzes incoming
Requests for Proposal (RFPs) and structures the responsive bid. It is the
go-to reference for the pre-construction estimating team and for the AI
copilot Scope Analysis Agent. The playbook captures the patterns we have
learned across hundreds of mechanical bids on data centers, hospitals,
pharmaceutical and life-science labs, stadiums, commercial offices,
education campuses, and industrial facilities.

## 1. RFP Review Checklist

Within 24 hours of receiving an RFP, the assigned senior estimator runs the
following checklist. Any unanswered item must be resolved before estimating
hours are charged to the bid.

1. **Owner identity confirmed** — name, parent organization, project
   sponsor, and AEC team of record.
2. **Project address and site logistics** — physical address, site access
   constraints, working hours, parking, and laydown.
3. **Scope documents** — drawings (latest revision), specifications,
   schedule milestones, and any narrative descriptions.
4. **Contract type** — lump sum, GMP, cost-plus, design-assist, or
   design-build mechanical.
5. **Commercial terms** — LDs, retention, payment terms, indemnity,
   warranty, bond requirements.
6. **Schedule** — NTP, substantial completion, phasing milestones, and any
   long-lead equipment delivery dates.
7. **Labor posture** — union vs. open-shop, prevailing wage, project labor
   agreement.
8. **Safety posture** — owner-specific safety program, drug testing,
   site-specific training.
9. **Bid due date and Q&A deadline.**
10. **Past-performance match** — confirm ACCO has at least three comparable
    projects in the past 5 years.

## 2. Common Exclusions

Estimators copy the relevant exclusions verbatim into the response. The
ACCO Estimating Standards Manual section 4 is the canonical list. The
playbook adds RFP-context exclusions:

- Coordination drawings beyond what ACCO's BIM team produces directly
- Trenching, dewatering, and underground utility relocation
- Cutting, patching, fireproofing, and fire-stopping
- Painting beyond factory finish coats
- Electrical work beyond final connections
- Roofing curbs and flashing
- Asbestos, lead, mold, or hazardous-material abatement
- Permits other than mechanical, plumbing, and refrigeration
- Builder's risk insurance and excess umbrella above ACCO standard
- Cybersecurity hardening of BMS controllers beyond manufacturer defaults
- Owner-furnished long-lead equipment storage beyond 30 days

When an exclusion is sensitive (e.g. an owner demands inclusion of cutting
and patching), elevate to the Chief Estimator before declining the
exclusion in writing.

## 3. Clarification Templates

Clarifications protect ACCO when the RFP is silent or ambiguous. Common
clarification language:

- "Bid pricing is valid for 30 days from the submission date."
- "Equipment escalation beyond 6 months from NTP is the customer's risk."
- "Working hours assumed Monday–Friday, 6 AM to 3 PM. Overtime, premium
  time, and weekend work are excluded."
- "Site access is assumed to be continuous from NTP through substantial
  completion. Phased or interrupted access will be priced via change
  order."
- "BMS programming assumes a single sequence of operations revision after
  initial submittal."
- "Commissioning scope is limited to the systems installed under this
  contract. Owner-furnished or other-trade systems are excluded from T&B
  and IQ/OQ scope."
- "Mechanical permit fees are based on the most recent jurisdiction
  schedule and are passed through at cost. Plan-review delays beyond 30
  days are the customer's risk."

## 4. Risk Flags

When the AI copilot or an estimator surfaces an RFP risk, classify it on
the three-tier scale and recommend a mitigation.

### 4.1 High-severity flags

- LDs greater than $10,000 per day with no cap.
- Unilateral termination-for-convenience clauses with less than 10% of
  contract value as the termination fee.
- Owner-furnished long-lead equipment without firm delivery commitment.
- Substantial completion before realistic critical-path duration.
- Live-occupied data hall or healthcare phasing with no off-shift premium
  budgeted.
- Pay-when-paid clauses tied to disputes that ACCO does not control.

### 4.2 Medium-severity flags

- Retention greater than 10% or no reduction at 50% complete.
- Ambiguous scope between mechanical and electrical for VFDs, BMS
  controllers, and switchgear.
- Unionized site without a posted PLA — confirm hours, fringes, and rules.
- Cleanroom or vivarium scope with no defined classification or pressure
  cascade.
- BMS integration with an undefined third-party building system.

### 4.3 Low-severity flags

- Customer-mandated reporting cadence (weekly or daily).
- Required attendance at owner OAC meetings beyond the standard cadence.
- Non-standard submittal format.
- Owner-mandated supplier list for specific commodity items.

## 5. Win Themes

Every ACCO proposal leads with a brief Win Themes section before the
technical narrative. Win themes are not generic — they target the owner's
stated drivers from the RFP. Strong win themes for ACCO include:

- **One-stop accountability** — ACCO self-performs HVAC, piping, plumbing,
  controls, and commissioning, eliminating coordination gaps and finger-
  pointing across trades.
- **Local self-perform craft** — ACCO craft labor in your region with deep
  experience on similar facilities.
- **Schedule certainty through prefabrication** — ACCO's prefab shops in
  Glendale and Las Vegas allow up to 60% of mechanical scope to be built
  off-site, accelerating schedule and reducing site labor density.
- **Safety record** — Multi-million-hour safety record without lost-time
  incident on data center, hospital, and stadium scope.
- **AI-assisted estimating** — ACCO uses the ACCO Estimator Assistant to
  pull comparable-project data and labor productivity at bid time,
  improving estimate accuracy and reducing carry.

## 6. Proposal Structure

Every ACCO proposal follows the same outline so reviewers can compare
across firms quickly:

1. Cover letter and executive summary
2. Win themes
3. Project understanding
4. Scope inclusions
5. Scope exclusions
6. Clarifications and assumptions
7. Schedule
8. Safety approach
9. Key personnel and project organization
10. Bid form / commercial summary

## 7. Submission and Follow-up

ACCO submits proposals 24 hours before the stated due date when possible
to allow for late questions from the owner. After submission, the senior
estimator owns the follow-up: a phone call within 2 business days, an
in-person presentation if requested, and a debrief request whether the
bid is awarded or not. Loss debriefs are logged into the pre-construction
database so the post-job analysis can compare lost-bid factors over time.

## 8. Document Control

This playbook is owned by the VP Pre-Construction and is reviewed
quarterly. Field input is encouraged from project managers who see the
downstream consequences of bid-time assumptions.
