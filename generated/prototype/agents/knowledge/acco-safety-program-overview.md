# ACCO Safety Program Overview

**Organization:** ACCO Engineered Systems
**Industry:** Mechanical Construction & Facility Services
**Last Updated:** May 18, 2026
**Classification:** Internal Use

---

## Overview

Safety is the first item on every ACCO Engineered Systems project agenda
and the first section in every ACCO proposal. ACCO has earned a safety
record that has been recognized by customers, general contractors, and
industry associations across the western United States. This document
summarizes the ACCO Safety Program at the level required to support
proposal narratives, bid clarifications, and project planning.

For full procedural detail, refer to the ACCO Safety Manual maintained by
the Director of Safety. The Safety Manual is the controlled document.
This overview is the briefing version used inside the ACCO Estimator
Assistant.

## 1. Safety Culture

ACCO's safety culture is grounded in three commitments:

1. **Every employee returns home injury-free.** Safety performance is a
   condition of employment for every craft worker, supervisor, and manager.
2. **Stop-work authority for everyone.** Any ACCO employee or
   subcontractor may stop work at any time without penalty if conditions
   become unsafe. Supervisors are required to support stop-work calls.
3. **Continuous improvement through honest reporting.** Near-misses are
   reported as aggressively as incidents. Lessons learned circulate
   weekly across the field organization.

These commitments are reflected in the ACCO Safety Pledge that every new
hire signs and in the daily pre-task plans required on every active job.

## 2. OSHA Compliance

ACCO operates as a fully OSHA-compliant contractor in every state where we
work. The program meets or exceeds 29 CFR 1926 (construction) standards
and addresses state-specific addenda such as Cal/OSHA Title 8. The
program is reviewed annually by the Director of Safety and by an
independent third-party auditor every three years.

Key OSHA-aligned program elements include:

- Hazard communication and SDS management
- Fall protection above six feet (Cal/OSHA threshold)
- Lockout / tagout procedures for energized systems
- Hot work permits for cutting, welding, and brazing
- Confined-space entry permits and atmospheric monitoring
- Respiratory protection program with annual fit testing
- Hearing conservation program
- Bloodborne pathogen exposure plan
- Crane and aerial lift operator qualification

## 3. Jobsite Procedures

Every active ACCO jobsite operates under the following procedures:

### 3.1 Daily pre-task planning

Every crew completes a daily pre-task plan (PTP) at the start of the shift.
The PTP identifies the day's specific tasks, hazards, and controls and is
signed by every crew member. PTPs are audited weekly by site supervision.

### 3.2 Toolbox talks

A toolbox talk is conducted weekly on every site. Topic selection is
risk-weighted to the upcoming scope. Attendance is logged.

### 3.3 Inspections

The site superintendent conducts a documented walk-through every workday.
A formal inspection by the regional safety manager occurs at least
monthly. Owner or General Contractor inspections are accommodated with
full transparency.

### 3.4 Critical-lift planning

Any mechanical lift over 75% of crane capacity, any tandem lift, or any
lift over an occupied area requires a written critical lift plan signed
by the rigging foreman, the superintendent, and the safety manager.

### 3.5 Live-system work

Live data center hot-aisle work, occupied hospital scope, and energized
electrical-adjacent work are governed by a site-specific plan reviewed
with the customer's facilities team and the GC. ACCO's default posture is
to de-energize and isolate whenever the schedule allows.

## 4. Training

Every craft worker completes the following training milestones:

- New-hire orientation (4 hours) covering ACCO safety expectations
- OSHA 10 (general industry construction) within 30 days of hire
- OSHA 30 for foremen and superintendents within 90 days of promotion
- Site-specific orientation on every new project before commencing work
- Annual refresher for fall protection, LOTO, and respiratory protection
- Confined-space, hot-work, and aerial-lift certifications as scope requires

Subcontractors must demonstrate equivalent training and provide
certifications before working on an ACCO-managed site.

## 5. Reporting

ACCO uses a single safety reporting platform across all regions. Every
incident, near-miss, first aid, and observation is logged within 24 hours.
The platform feeds the Director of Safety's weekly leading- and lagging-
indicator dashboard.

### 5.1 Leading indicators

- Number of safety observations per 100 craft hours
- Pre-task plan completion rate
- Toolbox-talk attendance rate
- Stop-work calls (these are tracked positively, not negatively)
- Audit findings closed within commitment dates

### 5.2 Lagging indicators

- OSHA recordable rate
- Days-away-restricted-or-transferred (DART) rate
- Experience modification rate (EMR)
- Lost-time incident rate

ACCO's three-year average EMR is well below the industry benchmark and is
shared with customers in pre-qualification submissions.

## 6. Customer and Bid Posture

For every bid, the ACCO Safety Program supports the proposal in three
ways:

1. **Inclusion language.** Every ACCO proposal explicitly includes the
   safety program, daily PTPs, weekly toolbox talks, and site-specific
   orientation.
2. **Exclusion language.** ACCO excludes owner-specific safety programs
   beyond OSHA 30 / Cal/OSHA equivalence unless explicitly priced.
3. **Clarification language.** ACCO clarifies that high-hazard scope
   (live data hall, energized work, confined-space welding) is priced
   with a productivity factor that reflects the additional control
   measures required.

The Scope Analysis Agent in the ACCO Estimator Assistant uses this
section to recommend safety-related clarifications and risk flags when
analyzing an RFP.
